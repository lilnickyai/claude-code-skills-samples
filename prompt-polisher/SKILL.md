# Prompt Polisher

Five minutes before you hit send. Most bad AI answers are not
the tool's fault. They are a vague prompt's fault. If your
prompt tries to do three jobs at once, never says who it is
for, and never says what a good answer looks like, you get a
generic answer back. This skill fixes that, one prompt at a
time, with no jargon and no prompt engineering course.

## Who this is for

Small business owners, realtors, teachers, anyone who uses
ChatGPT, Claude, or Grok and keeps getting answers that are
close but not quite right. No app required. A phone note
works fine.

## The 5 minute routine

### 1. One job, one sentence (1 minute)

Write what you want in one sentence. If you cannot, your
prompt is doing two jobs. Pick the one that matters now and
save the other for a second prompt.

Weak: "Help me with my business and marketing and social media."
Polished: "Write a 3 sentence Facebook post for first time home
buyers in Brunswick, GA, announcing an open house on Saturday."

One job beats three wishes every time.

### 2. Say who it is for and what shape the answer takes (2 minutes)

Add two lines to your prompt: who will read the answer, and
what form it should come in. Length, format, and tone are not
extras. They are the difference between a wall of text and
something you can actually use.

Try this template:

"Write this for [who]. Give me [format, for example a short
list, a draft email, or 3 options]. Keep it under [length]."

Example: "Write this for busy parents. Give me a 5 item
checklist. Keep each item under 10 words."

If you skip this step, the tool guesses your audience and your
format. It will guess wrong about half the time.

### 3. Cut the filler, add one concrete detail (1 minute)

Delete the throat clearing. "I was just wondering if maybe you
could possibly help me with..." adds nothing. Start with the
job.

Then add one concrete detail the tool cannot guess: a price,
a date, a place, an example of what good looks like. One detail
does more work than five adjectives.

Weak: "Write something nice about my shop."
Polished: "Write a 2 sentence welcome sign for a bakery that
opens at 7 AM and sells out of cinnamon rolls by 9."

### 4. The stranger test (1 minute)

Read your polished prompt once and ask: if a stranger read
only this, would they know exactly what to do? If the answer
is no, the missing piece is usually the audience, the format,
or the one concrete detail. Add that piece and send it.

If the answer is still off after one polished try, do not
rewrite the whole prompt again. Reply with one line: "Shorter,
and give me an example." One correction beats a full restart.

## What this skill does not do

It does not teach prompt engineering, build a prompt library,
or rewrite your voice. Writing in your own voice has its own
skill. This one only cleans up the prompt in front of you, so
the next answer lands closer to what you actually wanted.

It also does not fix a bad idea with better wording. If you
do not know what you want yet, decide that first. A polished
prompt for the wrong job just gets you the wrong answer
faster.
