# Follow-Up Tracker

One list for everything waiting on someone else. Not a to-do list. A waiting list.

## What it does

You tell your agent what is open, who it is waiting on, and when you want to check back. The agent keeps one short list, reminds you of one nudge a day, and closes a loop the moment it is answered. Nothing stays in your head, and nothing gets chased twice in one day.

## The routine

### 1. Catch the loop in one line

When something is waiting on someone, add one line:

Who it is waiting on. What you are waiting for. When to check back.

Example: Waiting on Sam. The catering quote. Check back Thursday.

If there is no date, the check back date is three days from today.

### 2. One daily check, one nudge

Once a day, at a time you pick, the agent reads the list back to you in three groups:

Due today. Due this week. No date yet.

Then it picks exactly one nudge: the oldest due item, or the one you name. It drafts the nudge in one short paragraph, polite and specific, with the original ask restated so the other person does not have to dig back through old messages.

You send it, or you say skip, and the loop stays open with a new check back date.

### 3. Close it the moment it lands

When an answer arrives, the loop closes. The agent removes it from the list and notes what the answer was, in one line, so you can find it later without rereading the whole thread.

A loop with no answer after two nudges gets one decision, not a third nudge: drop it, call instead, or set a new date. Chasing the same person a third time by message is rarely what moves it.

## Worked example

Monday: you ask a supplier for a price. Loop added. Waiting on supplier. Price for 50 flyers. Check back Wednesday.

Wednesday: the daily check shows it due today. The agent drafts: "Hi, just checking on the price for 50 flyers we talked about Monday. No rush if it is still in progress, just wanted to confirm you got it." You send it.

Thursday: the supplier replies with the price. The loop closes. Note saved: Supplier price, 50 flyers, $180.

Friday: the daily check shows nothing due. No nudge. A quiet list is a working list.

## Guardrails

Keep the list short. If it grows past 15 open loops, the agent asks you to close or drop three before adding more. A list nobody reads is just a second inbox.

One nudge a day, maximum. Never nudge the same person twice in one day, and never nudge on a loop that is not yet due.

Do not guess what someone meant. If an answer is unclear, the agent asks you one clarifying question before closing the loop.

This skill tracks loops. It does not write full emails for you, turn meeting notes into action items, or plan your whole day. Those are separate jobs, and keeping this one small is why it works.

## Try it today

Add three loops you are actually waiting on right now. Set one check back date for each. Tomorrow, do the one daily check, send the one nudge, and notice how much lighter the mental load feels when the waiting has a place to live.
