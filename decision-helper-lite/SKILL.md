# Decision Helper Lite

For one decision, two options, ten minutes. Not a big analysis.
You pick one thing, you check it three ways, you walk away with a
choice and a first step.

This is the lite version on purpose. It handles one decision at a
time. If you have a whole list of decisions, run it once per decision,
starting with the one that is blocking everything else.

## Who this is for

Small business owners, realtors, teachers, creators, anyone staring
at two options and going in circles. No jargon, no spreadsheet
required. A pen and a phone note work fine.

## The 10 minute routine

### 1. Say it in one line (1 minute)

Write the decision as one sentence with exactly two options:

"Should I do A, or should I do B?"

If you have three or more options, cross out the weakest one first.
If you only have one option, your second option is "do nothing for
now." That counts, and it is often the right answer.

Also write down the deadline. A decision with no deadline just sits
there. If there is no real deadline, set one: "I decide by Friday
at noon."

### 2. Score it three ways (5 minutes)

For each option, give it a score from 1 to 5 on three things only:

- **Money and time cost.** 5 means cheap and quick, 1 means expensive and slow.
- **How much it helps.** 5 means it clearly moves the goal forward, 1 means it barely matters.
- **How easy it is to undo.** 5 means you can back out tomorrow, 1 means you are stuck with it.

Add the three scores. The higher total usually wins, but do not
stop there. Scores lie when one factor matters way more than the
others. If one option wins on cost and help but is impossible to
undo, say that out loud before you pick it.

Example, a realtor choosing between two open house weekends:

- Weekend A: cost 4, help 4, undo 5. Total 13.
- Weekend B: cost 2, help 5, undo 3. Total 10.

Weekend A wins, even though B might bring slightly more foot
traffic, because A is cheaper, faster, and easy to change.

### 3. The gut check and the tiny test (4 minutes)

Ask two questions:

- "If I had to pick in the next 30 seconds, which one would I pick?"
  That first answer is data, not magic. If it matches the scores,
  you are done. If it fights the scores, one of your scores is
  wrong. Find which one.
- "What is the smallest test I can run this week?" Pick the version
  of the choice you can try for one day, one customer, or one post,
  then judge it on real results instead of guessing.

Then write down:

```
Decision: [one line from step 1]
Pick: [A or B]
Why in one sentence: [the reason that actually tipped it]
First step: [the smallest thing you do today]
Test by: [date, one week out at most]
```

That is the whole skill. A decision you can explain in one
sentence, with a first step you can do today, beats a perfect
decision you never make.

## What this lite version does not do

It does not compare five options at once, it does not dig into
long term tradeoffs, and it does not handle high stakes calls like
hiring, signing a lease, or spending money you cannot afford to
lose. Those deserve a slower look and a second opinion from a real
person. This lite version is for the everyday stuck choices that
are eating your week.
