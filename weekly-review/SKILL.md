# Weekly Review

The daily plan handles today. This skill makes sure the days add up to something. Once a week, usually Friday afternoon or Sunday evening, sit down with your agent for 20 minutes and close the week properly: what got done, what did not, and what next week is actually for.

Skipped reviews are how "busy" turns into "nothing shipped." This one is short on purpose. Twenty minutes, same steps every time.

This skill is free. If it earns its keep, the full productivity collection is waiting at the end.

## The core idea: close the loop

Every week produces three things: finished work, unfinished work, and lessons. Most people carry all three in their head into Monday, which is why Monday feels heavy. The review moves them out of your head and into a written record. Next week starts light.

## The 20-minute routine

Walk the user through this once a week, in this order. Do not skip steps; each one feeds the next.

**1. Score the week (5 minutes).** Pull up last week's plan: the top threes from the daily-plan skill, or whatever list was used. For each planned priority, mark it done, moved, or dropped.

Done is done. Moved means it carries into next week with a reason. Dropped means it is officially let go, not "getting to it later." Later is how tasks become ghosts.

Have the agent count: "How many of the top priorities actually finished?" No judgment in the number. The number is just information.

**2. Collect the strays (5 minutes).** Ask: "What came up this week that is not on any list?" Loose ends, promises made in passing, ideas scribbled somewhere. Capture every one and give each a home: next week's list, a someday list, or the trash. Everything gets a verdict. Nothing stays as "I will deal with it."

**3. Find the pattern (5 minutes).** Ask the agent: "What kept getting in the way this week?" Look at the moved and dropped items. The pattern is usually one of three: too many priorities, interruptions eating the deep work block, or priorities that were never really priorities.

Name the pattern out loud. One sentence. "I planned five big things and had time for two." Then pick one fix for next week, not three. One.

**4. Set next week's big three (5 minutes).** Not the daily top three. The weekly three: the three outcomes that would make next week a win. Write them where Monday morning will see them.

Then ask: "What is the one thing most likely to derail this?" Name it, and decide the counter move now. A plan with a named risk beats a plan with crossed fingers.

## The example

Show the user what a finished review looks like. Concrete beats abstract.

> WEEK SCORED: 7 of 9 priorities finished
> MOVED: dentist reschedule (third week in a row, just call Monday 9am)
> DROPPED: reorganize downloads folder (not a real priority, admit it)
> STRAYS CAPTURED: 4 (2 to next week, 1 to someday, 1 trashed)
> PATTERN: afternoons evaporate into messages, deep work only happens before 10am
> FIX: block 8-10am daily, phone in the other room
> NEXT WEEK'S BIG THREE:
> 1. Finish Q3 report and send to Priya
> 2. Call dentist Monday 9am, actually book it
> 3. Approve Marco's invoice before the Friday deadline
> RISK: the report will eat the week. Counter move: two mornings blocked, no meetings before 10.

Point out: the week is scored, the strays have homes, one pattern is named, next week has three outcomes and one named risk. Twenty minutes.

## Rules the agent enforces

Teach the agent these guardrails so the review stays useful:

- **Done, moved, or dropped.** Every planned item gets one of the three labels. "Still working on it" is not a label. It is either moving to next week or being dropped.
- **One pattern, one fix.** The review finds exactly one pattern and prescribes exactly one fix. A review that produces five action items is just more planning.
- **Write it down.** The review lives in a dated file or note. Memory is not a system. Next week's review opens with this week's record.
- **Protect the streak, not the perfection.** A scored week at 50 percent beats an unreviewed week every time. The point is the habit, not the score.

## The practice exercise

Run the review right now on the week that just ended, for real. Score it, collect the strays, name the pattern, set next week's three. Write it down. Then set a weekly reminder: same time, same place, every week.
