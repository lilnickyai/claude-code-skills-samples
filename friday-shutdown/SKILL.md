# Friday Shutdown

Ten minutes on Friday afternoon, then you stop. This is not a
review of your whole week and it is not a plan for next week.
It is the shutdown step: park what is open, write down the one
thing Monday starts with, and close the week so it stops
following you home.

If you skip it, open loops do not go away. They just move into
your weekend and sit there, half remembered, until Monday. Ten
minutes now buys you a weekend that is actually yours.

## Who this is for

Small business owners, realtors, teachers, contractors, anyone
whose work does not have a clean quitting time. No jargon, no
app required. A phone note works fine.

## The 10 minute routine

### 1. Park it, do not solve it (4 minutes)

List the open loops that are still buzzing in your head. Cap it
at three. For each one, write one line only: what it is, and the
next step. Do not start the step. Parking is the job.

- "Client quote: send the revised price Monday morning."
- "Supplier order: check the delivery date, not the whole order."
- "School form: sign it and put it in the bag Sunday night."

If a loop has no next step, that is the answer. Write "waiting
on them" and leave it there. A loop with no step is not yours
to carry over the weekend.

### 2. Send the one message that unblocks Monday (3 minutes)

Pick one message, at most, that keeps someone else from being
stuck while you are off. A short reply, a date confirmed, a
file sent. Not a catch-up, not a thread, one message.

If nothing is truly blocking anyone, skip this step. Sending
messages to feel productive is how a shutdown turns back into
work.

### 3. Write Monday's first step (2 minutes)

Write one sentence, in plain words, for what Monday starts
with. Not three priorities, not a to-do list. One step.

"Monday starts with the client quote."

Put it where Monday-you will see it first: the top of a note,
a sticky note, the first line of your planner. Monday-you
should not have to reconstruct Friday. Friday-you just did
that job.

### 4. Close it (1 minute)

Close the tabs, close the notebooks, put the work phone down
if you can. Then write one last line and mean it:

"Week parked. Monday starts with [your one step]."

That line is the shutdown. Once it is written, work questions
wait until Monday. If a new one pops up over the weekend,
write it on the park list and leave it there. Do not open it.

## What this skill does not do

It does not review your whole week, rank your priorities, or
plan next week's calendar. That is a bigger weekly review, and
it has its own skill. This one only parks the open loops and
names Monday's first step, so you can actually stop.

It also does not fix a week that is genuinely overloaded. If
your park list is ten items long every Friday, the problem is
not your shutdown routine. It is the load, and that needs a
real conversation about what gets dropped, not a better list.
