# Inbox Triage

Nobody gets to inbox zero by reading faster. You get there by deciding faster. This skill runs a 15-minute triage with an AI agent: every email gets one of four verdicts, and the inbox ends the session with nothing undecided in it. Reading comes later. Deciding comes now.

## Why triage, not "catch up"

"Catching up on email" fails because it mixes two jobs: deciding what matters and doing the work. The triage separates them. In this session the agent does not write replies, does not do tasks, does not fall down rabbit holes. It sorts. Fifteen minutes of pure sorting beats two hours of half-reading every time.

## The 15-minute routine

The user shares their inbox however they can: pasting subject lines, reading them aloud, or forwarding a batch. Walk through all four steps. Speed matters more than perfection; a fast wrong decision can be revisited, but an undecided email rots.

**1. The four verdicts (10 minutes).** For each email, the agent assigns exactly one:

- **DO**: needs a reply or action from the user today. Two minutes or less of work. These get flagged, not done. Doing starts after triage.
- **WAITING**: someone else owes the user something. Note what is owed and by whom. If it has been waiting more than a week, it becomes a DO: send the nudge.
- **FILE**: useful reference, no action. Newsletters worth keeping, receipts, confirmations. Out of the inbox, into a folder or archive.
- **DELETE**: everything else. Expired promos, notifications that were already handled, FYIs that informed no one. Most inboxes are 40 percent delete on the first triage. Say so. It is liberating, not wasteful.

No fifth option. "I'll look at it later" is not a verdict; it is how inboxes grow. If an email genuinely cannot be decided in ten seconds, it is a DO with the action "decide what this is."

**2. The DO list (2 minutes).** Read back every DO item as a plain list. The user should see the real shape of their day: usually 3 to 7 things, not the 200 the inbox suggested. This is the moment the overwhelm breaks. Name it: "Your inbox said 200. Your actual work is 5."

**3. The waiting check (2 minutes).** Read the WAITING list with names and dates. Anything older than a week gets one nudge, drafted now or scheduled. Waiting items are the silent killers; nobody follows up, so everything stalls politely.

**4. The unsubscribe sweep (1 minute).** During triage the agent notes every sender that earned a DELETE three times. Those are unsubscribe candidates. One click each, done after the session. The best inbox system is the one that receives less mail.

> DELETED (28)
>
> UNSUBSCRIBE CANDIDATES: daily-deals digest, two store promo lists

Point at the ratio. "Twenty-eight of forty-seven emails needed nothing from you. The inbox was lying about your workload."

## Rules the agent enforces

- **Decide, don't do.** The moment the agent starts drafting a reply mid-triage, the session is lost. Sorting first, doing after. Say this out loud at the start.
- **Ten seconds per email.** If it takes longer, the email is badly written, not important. Verdict it DO ("figure out what this wants") and move on.
- **Every email gets a verdict.** No skips, no "later" pile. The later pile is the old inbox wearing a new name.
- **Celebrate the delete count.** Read it back with energy. Deleting 28 emails is the win. Most people feel guilty; the skill teaches them to feel free.

## The practice exercise

Run the triage on the user's real inbox right now, even if it is not overflowing. Use their actual emails, real verdicts, real DO list. The first triage is always the messiest and the most valuable. End by writing the DO list somewhere the user will see it today.
