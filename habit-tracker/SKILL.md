# Habit Tracker

One habit, one mark a day. Most habit plans fail because
they start too big. Five habits at once, a perfect morning
routine, a full tracker spreadsheet. Then one busy day breaks
the streak and the whole plan goes in the bin. This skill
does the opposite. One habit, made small enough to do on a
bad day, marked once, and looked at once a week. No app
required. A phone note or a paper calendar works fine.

## Who this is for

Small business owners, creators, teachers, anyone who has
started a habit three times and dropped it three times. If
you have ever said "I was doing so well and then I just
stopped," this is for you.

## Pick one habit, and shrink it

Pick exactly one habit. Not three, not five. One.

Then shrink it until it feels almost too easy. The test is
simple: could you still do it on your worst day this week?
If the answer is no, it is still too big.

- "Walk every day" becomes "walk to the mailbox and back."
- "Read more" becomes "read one page before bed."
- "Drink more water" becomes "one glass with breakfast."
- "Post every day" becomes "write one sentence for tomorrow's post."

A habit you actually do beats a habit you admire. You can
make it bigger later, after two full weeks of daily marks.
Do not make it bigger in week one.

## The one mark a day

Every day, mark one thing: did you do it or not. That is all.

Use one line per day, always in the same place:

- Mon: yes
- Tue: yes
- Wed: no, sick day
- Thu: yes

A "no" is not a failure. It is data. Write one short reason
next to it if you know it: sick, traveling, forgot. No
reason is fine too. What matters is the mark, not the excuse.
Never backfill a week from memory on Sunday. If a day has no
mark, it stays blank, and you move on.

## The missed day rule

One missed day is normal. Two missed days in a row is the
danger point. That is when most people quietly quit.

So the rule is: never miss twice. After a missed day, the
next day's only job is to get the mark back. Do the smallest
version of the habit, even the mailbox version, and mark it.
You are not making up for yesterday. You are just restarting
the run.

If you miss a full week, do not restart the whole plan.
Shrink the habit again and start a fresh run. The habit was
too big, not you.

## The 5 minute weekly review

Once a week, same day each week, spend five minutes on three
questions:

1. How many marks did I get this week? Count them, no judgment.
2. What got in the way on the missed days, if any? Look for
   one pattern, not a full confession. "I always miss it on
   driving days" is a useful pattern.
3. One fix for next week, or none. A fix means changing the
   time, the place, or the size of the habit. "Do it right
   after breakfast instead of at night" is a fix. "Try
   harder" is not a fix.

If you got seven marks, keep the habit exactly as it is for
another week. If you got four or fewer, shrink it again
before you blame yourself.

## What this skill is not

This is not a morning routine, a full day plan, or a life
overhaul. It tracks one habit, nothing else. It does not
plan your week, organize your work, or fix an overloaded
schedule. One habit, one mark, one weekly look. That is the
whole system, and that is why it holds up.

## Get started today

Pick your one habit now, shrink it to the worst day version,
and write today's mark before bed. One line. That is day one.
