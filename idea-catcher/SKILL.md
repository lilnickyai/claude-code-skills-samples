# Idea Catcher

Thirty seconds now, five minutes on Sunday. Most lost ideas are not bad ideas. They are good ideas caught at the wrong time, in the wrong place, with no next step attached. So they live in your head, nag you all day, and vanish by night. This skill fixes that, with one place to put them and one weekly look at them. No app required. A phone note works fine.

## Who this is for

Small business owners, creators, teachers, anyone who gets ideas while driving, cooking, showering, or working on something else. If you have ever said "I had a great idea earlier and now it is gone," this is for you.

## The 30 second catch

When an idea shows up, do not judge it, do not plan it, and do not open a new project for it. Just catch four short lines in one note, always in the same place:

1. The idea, in one sentence. "A printable checklist for first time home buyers."
2. What sparked it. "A buyer asked the same three questions at the open house."
3. Who it is for. "First time buyers" is enough.
4. One next step, if one is obvious. "Ask two buyers what they wish they had known." If none is obvious, write "none yet" and move on.

That is the whole catch. Thirty seconds, then back to what you were doing. An idea you caught badly beats an idea you lost perfectly.

Pick one place and stick to it: one phone note, one page in a notebook, one doc. Ideas scattered across five apps are ideas lost with extra steps. If you catch one on paper, move it into your one place that same day.

## The 5 minute weekly review

Once a week, Sunday works well, open your one place and do three things:

1. Read the week's catches once, fast. Do not edit them.
2. Pick one to move forward, or pick none. Picking none is a fine week. Most ideas are fine to keep parked.
3. For the one you picked, write its very next step and when you will do it. Not a plan, just a step. "Draft the checklist Tuesday morning" beats "launch a product line."

Everything else stays parked. A parked idea is not a failed idea. It is an idea waiting for its week.

## What this skill does not do

It does not turn an idea into a product, write your outline, or price anything. Turning messy notes into a product outline has its own skill, and that is where the building starts. This one only catches ideas and keeps them findable, so the building skill has something real to work with later.

It also does not fix having too many ideas. If your list grows every week and nothing ever moves, the fix is not a better list. Pick one, do its first step, and let the rest wait. A short list you use beats a long list you admire.
